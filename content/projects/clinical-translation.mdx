## Problem
Nepali is a low-resource language, and medical translation is unforgiving: a wrong term can mean a wrong diagnosis. General translation systems lack the parallel medical data to get clinical terminology right.

## Approach
- Curated and cleaned a domain-specific English–Nepali parallel corpus of **70k+** entries, combining several sources with medical relevance.
- Fine-tuned two multilingual models, **mBART** and **NLLB-200**, in PyTorch under one experimental framework.
- Evaluated with six measures so that surface overlap and meaning were both covered: BLEU, chrF++, METEOR, BERTScore, COMET, and perplexity.
- Built dashboards to compare models across metrics.

## Findings
- NLLB-200 outperformed mBART on both surface-level and semantic metrics.
- NLLB-200 also produced fewer hallucinations on clinical text.
- Error profiling surfaced term omissions and cultural bias as the main remaining risks.

## Responsible use
Because this is medical text, the work included an ethics assessment covering safety, fairness, and deployment constraints, not just scores.

## What I'd improve
- A clinician-reviewed human evaluation rubric for terminology.
- Confidence estimates so the system can flag translations it shouldn't be trusted on.
