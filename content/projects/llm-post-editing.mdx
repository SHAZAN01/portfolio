## Problem
Translation models trained on general text get worse when they meet a new domain: medical notes, legal language, technical manuals. The usual fix is to fine-tune a copy of the model for each domain. That costs data, compute, and a deployment per domain.

## Idea
Leave the translation model untouched. It produces a draft, and a large language model edits that draft with the target domain in mind. Adaptation happens at test time, in the editing step, instead of in the weights.

## How it was tested
The frozen-model-plus-editor set-up was compared against a fully fine-tuned model on the same four domains.

## Result
Across four domains, LLM post-editing recovered **87–92%** of the improvement a fully fine-tuned model achieved, without any retraining.

## Why it matters
For teams that can't maintain one fine-tuned model per domain, a frozen model plus an editor is a cheaper path that keeps most of the quality.
